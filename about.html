Agent Foundry is one person

I am Muhammad Arsalan, and I build the thing I sell you.

No agency layer, no account manager, no junior handed the work after you sign. The person who scopes your project is the person who writes it, tests it, puts it live and fixes it at 11pm when something breaks.

What I actually do

I build AI agents for small service businesses: the kind where the phone ringing is the business, and a missed call is a job that went to whoever picked up instead. A phone agent that answers at 2am and books into your real calendar. A text that goes out four seconds after a missed call. An outbound system that stops every remaining step the moment someone replies.

They are not chatbots bolted onto a website. Each one does a whole job end to end and hands the odd case to a person, and each one ships with a test suite and an eval set you can run yourself.

How I got here

Seven years of my career were spent inside other people's operations rather than at arm's length from them, at TPS Worldwide, Meezan Bank, Folio3 and Staples Canada. The job was always the same shape: learn how the work really runs, find the step where it breaks, and say what to do about it.

That turns out to be most of this job too. The hard part of an AI agent is not the model. It is knowing that your price list has three exceptions nobody wrote down, that Tuesday mornings are different, and that the thing your receptionist does when a caller is upset is not in any process document. Models are easy to make talk. Making one hold up on a busy Monday, with real customers and messy data, is the work.

What I have not done yet

I would rather you hear this from me than find out on the call.

I have built three of these agents end to end and tested them hard, but I have not yet run one on a paying client's live phone line. The numbers on this site come from my own test and evaluation runs, never from a client's business results, and you will not find a logo wall here because there isn't one to show.

What that buys you is a lower price than this work costs from an agency, and an engineer who needs your project to go well more than he needs your money. What it costs you is the comfort of going second. If that trade is wrong for you, it is genuinely better that we both know now.

How I work

  • It lives in your accounts. Vapi, Twilio, your calendar, your CRM, all in your name with your own cards. Usage is billed to you at cost and I never resell minutes. If I disappeared, it would keep running and the bills would keep arriving as normal.
  • You own the code. Every line, handed over, with the runbook. No licence, no platform you are locked into, no monthly fee to keep your own agent switched on.
  • Prices come from a list you approved. The single most common way these things fail is an agent inventing a quote. Mine read from a list stored outside the model, and say someone will call back when a question falls outside it.
  • Straight answers. If an off-the-shelf tool solves your problem for forty dollars a month, that is what I will tell you, and I will name the tool.

Where I am, and what that means for you

I work from Karachi, Pakistan, which is UTC+5. That puts my afternoon in the UK's morning and my evening in the US east coast's morning, so calls are booked between 9am and 1pm or 5pm and 10pm my time and most people find a slot without thinking about it. Everything is remote: calls, shared documents, and a number you can ring to hear the thing working.

Clients so far have come through Upwork, where I am Top Rated with five stars on every contract. Those are quality-engineering engagements rather than agent builds, which is the honest version of that sentence.

Where to start
