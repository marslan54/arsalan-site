What can you automate?

Mostly the work around leads and customers: answering calls, texts and website chats, following up, booking appointments, outbound email, recruiting outreach, and moving data between your tools. If a task repeats and follows rules, it can usually be automated, with a person approving the steps that matter.

Which tools do you work with?

n8n for workflows; Claude, OpenAI and Gemini for the AI; Vapi and Twilio for calls and SMS; HubSpot, GoHighLevel, Clay, Apollo and Instantly for sales; Cal.com, Slack and Gmail; and TypeScript, Python and Postgres when a project needs real code. The full list is on the services page.

How do you stop an AI agent from going wrong?

The same way I tested banking software. Every agent ships with automated tests, and conversational ones are put through simulated customers (price pressure, prompt injection, off-topic requests) and graded before launch. They only state facts they were given, never invent prices, and hand over to a person when they should.

How long does a project take?

It depends on the scope: a single automation can take days, a full agent with integrations a few weeks. You get the timeline with the quote, before any work starts.

Where are you, and when can we talk?

I'm in Karachi, Pakistan, on UTC+5 all year, and calls run 9am–1pm and 5pm–10pm my time, Monday to Friday. That's mornings for the US East Coast, afternoons for the UK and Europe, both ends of the working day in the Gulf, and afternoons in Australia. The booking page shows the times in your own timezone, so you don't have to work it out.

What happens to what I type in the chat?

It's used only to answer you and, if you ask, to pass your details to me. Nothing is sold or used for advertising. The privacy policy lists exactly what's stored and how to have it deleted.
