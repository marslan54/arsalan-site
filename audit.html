The five days

Five days on my calendar. Two calls on yours.

  1. Day 1

    The deep dive

    Sixty to ninety minutes on how your business actually runs: where the hours go, what your team types twice, what you have already tried and why it stopped. You talk, I map.

  2. Day 2

    Both workflows mapped

    Every step of your two costliest workflows written down, including the steps nobody mentions on a call because they are too obvious to say out loud. That is usually where the cost hides.

  3. Day 3

    The work priced

    A monthly number on each one: time at your real wage cost, what the mistakes cost, and what the delay costs you in work that never got quoted. Your numbers where you have them, stated assumptions where you do not.

  4. Day 4

    Ranked, and the plan written

    Every candidate ordered by return against effort, and the first 30 days written out. This is also where I rule things out, which is often the more valuable half, and it is the day your demo number goes live.

  5. Day 5

    The walkthrough

    About forty-five minutes. I take you through the sheet and the plan and answer every question. You keep both, plus the recording.

Before you book

The questions people actually ask.

Have you actually done this before?

Yes. Seven years of it. I have spent my career as a forward-deployed engineer, sitting inside other people’s operations at TPS Worldwide, Meezan Bank, Folio3 and Staples Canada: learning how the work really flows, finding the step where it breaks, and saying what to do about it. That is this audit, with a fixed scope and a report at the end. On top of that I have built three of these agents end to end, so the costs and timelines I put against a build come from having done the build.

Will you just tell me to hire you for a build?

No. The sheet follows your numbers. If a $40-a-month off-the-shelf tool solves your problem, that is what the plan will say, and I will name the tool. I would rather lose a build than have you pay me for one that does not pay you back.

What do you need from me?

Time for two calls, the first about 60 to 90 minutes and the walkthrough about 45. Rough numbers on team size and volumes if you have them. No logins, and no access to your systems.

Is the demo number a real agent, or a recording?

It is the same voice agent I build for clients, on a real number, loaded with your services and prices. Ask it something it has no answer for and it should say so rather than invent one, which is the behaviour worth testing. The number is mine and it comes down at the end of the audit week. If you go ahead with a build, the agent moves into your own Twilio and Vapi accounts.

What if my problem is bigger than two workflows?

Then I start with the two most expensive ones, because that is where the money is. If the walkthrough shows you need a wider map than that, I will tell you on the call and quote it separately rather than quietly running over.

What if the honest answer is to automate nothing?

Then that is what the walkthrough says, and it is a useful answer. Plenty of businesses are too small, too varied or too seasonal for this to pay back yet. Knowing that for $400 is cheaper than finding out for $3,500.

How does the refund work?

Say so on the walkthrough call and I refund the full $400. No form and no argument. You keep the sheet and the plan either way.

Does the $400 come off a build?

Yes. If you go ahead with a build within 60 days, the $400 comes off the price of it.
